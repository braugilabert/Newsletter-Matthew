KEY 01

Neuropsychology case files

Answers, explanations and one more small step.

1.1 · C. Anterograde declarative amnesia; hippocampal memory systems

Bilateral medial temporal damage can severely disrupt new declarative learning while sparing aspects of immediate memory and procedural learning.

Worked example / practice: Remembering how to ride a bicycle but forgetting meeting the instructor illustrates different memory systems.

Explore the topic

1.2 · A. Prosopagnosia; ventral occipitotemporal face-processing network

A selective face-recognition difficulty despite adequate basic vision suggests prosopagnosia. Right-sided or bilateral ventral visual-network damage may be involved.

Worked example / practice: Recognising a friend by their voice while failing to recognise their face helps distinguish a face-processing problem from a general memory problem.

Explore the topic

1.3 · A. Associative visual agnosia; ventral visual recognition pathways

Preserved copying suggests enough perceptual processing to form a representation, while access to object meaning from vision is impaired. Naming and semantic abilities must also be assessed.

Worked example / practice: If the same object can be recognised by touch, the difficulty is more specific to visual recognition than to loss of all object knowledge.

Explore the topic

KEY 02

Neuroscience

Answers, explanations and one more small step.

2.1 · A. Locus coeruleus

The locus coeruleus projects widely and contributes to arousal, attention and responses to salient events.

Worked example / practice: Use a pairing card: locus coeruleus -> noradrenaline. Raphe nuclei -> serotonin is another useful pairing.

Explore the topic

2.2 · B. Forming a new memory of meeting someone yesterday

The hippocampus supports new episodic and relational learning. It is not the storage site for every kind of memory.

Worked example / practice: Remembering an event and improving at a motor skill can dissociate, showing that memory is not one single system.

Explore the topic

2.3 · B. Learning about biologically significant cues and contributing to emotional responses

Amygdala circuits contribute to emotional learning and salience, including but not limited to threat processing.

Worked example / practice: A previously neutral sound can acquire significance through learning. Several connected structures contribute to the response.

Explore the topic

KEY 03

Psychotherapy toolkit

Answers, explanations and one more small step.

3.1 · B. A behavioural experiment

A behavioural experiment gathers evidence about a specific prediction. It is more than simply telling the client to stop worrying.

Worked example / practice: Prediction: 'Everyone will laugh.' Observable outcome: how people actually respond to one ordinary question.

Explore the topic

3.2 · B. Scheduling a small meaningful activity and reviewing its effect

Behavioural activation addresses withdrawal and reduced reinforcement through planned engagement. Activities are selected collaboratively and adjusted to circumstances.

Worked example / practice: A small plan might be a ten-minute walk on Tuesday, followed by a brief note about mood and sense of achievement.

Explore the topic

3.3 · B. What evidence supports this thought, and what evidence complicates it?

Cognitive reappraisal examines accuracy and usefulness. It does not assume distress is irrational or that real difficulties can be wished away.

Worked example / practice: 'I failed one exam' is specific evidence. 'I will fail everything forever' is a much broader prediction.

Explore the topic

KEY 04

A little Gaeilge

Answers, explanations and one more small step.

4.1 · B. Dia is Muire duit.

Dia is Muire duit is the traditional response to Dia duit. Other greetings and local forms are also used.

Worked example / practice: Two-person dialogue: A: Dia duit. B: Dia is Muire duit. A simple friendly greeting exchange.

Explore the topic

4.2 · A. a trí

A trí is three in the basic counting series. Counting people and counting objects introduce additional patterns.

Worked example / practice: Count aloud: a haon, a dó, a trí, a ceathair, a cúig. This is practice with the abstract counting series.

Explore the topic

4.3 · A. Cupán tae, le do thoil.

Le do thoil means please when speaking to one person. Le bhur dtoil is the plural form.

Worked example / practice: At a café: Ba mhaith liom cupán tae, le do thoil. Meaning: I would like a cup of tea, please.

Explore the topic

KEY 05

Music theory, finally

Answers, explanations and one more small step.

5.1 · B. One

A semitone is the smallest adjacent-key step in the usual twelve-note equal-tempered keyboard system.

Worked example / practice: E to F is one semitone even though both are white keys. C to D is two semitones.

Explore the topic

5.2 · B. Twelve

An octave spans twelve semitones in the twelve-note system, while its diatonic letter-name span counts eight notes inclusively.

Worked example / practice: C to the next higher C is an octave. The letter names C D E F G A B C count eight positions including both endpoints.

Explore the topic

5.3 · A. E G B D F

The treble clef identifies the G line, which is the second line from the bottom. The five lines are E G B D F.

Worked example / practice: The spaces from bottom to top spell F A C E. Compare lines and spaces rather than memorising them as one mixed list.

Explore the topic
