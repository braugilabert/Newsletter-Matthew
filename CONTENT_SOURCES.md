# Content sources and editorial notes

All quiz vignettes, distractors, explanations and worked examples were written for this project. Cases are fictional. Links provide background reading; they are not claims that a source has reviewed or endorsed the quiz bank.

## Neuropsychology and neuroscience

- [OpenStax: Introduction to Behavioral Neuroscience](https://openstax.org/details/books/introduction-behavioral-neuroscience): broad background for neural systems, methods, cognition and neurotransmission.
- [OpenStax: neurotransmitters made from amino acids](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-behavioral-neuroscience/pages/3-2-neurotransmitters-made-from-amino-acids): transmitter functions depend on pathways and receptors.
- [OpenStax: neural signalling](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-behavioral-neuroscience/pages/2-4-mechanisms-of-neural-signaling) and [bioelectricity](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-behavioral-neuroscience/pages/2-3-principles-of-bioelectricity): conductances, ion gradients, propagation and synapses.
- [OpenStax: reward circuitry](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-behavioral-neuroscience/pages/14-3-neural-circuitry-of-drug-reward): reward prediction errors and the limits of a pleasure-only dopamine account.
- [NIDCD: Aphasia](https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/health/aphasia): language syndromes, causes and limitations of simple localisation.
- [NCBI Bookshelf: Agnosia](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK493156/): recognition impairments and related clinical terminology.

Clinical items ask for the best explanation **within the information supplied**. They distinguish language, attention, visual fields, praxis and recognition, and avoid equating every function with one isolated brain spot. Epilepsy items include supporting EEG findings when lateralisation is relevant. Neurotransmitter items avoid labels such as dopamine = pleasure or oxytocin = universal trust.

## Psychotherapy

- [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Types of Treatment](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/16-2-types-of-treatment): overview of therapeutic traditions and learning principles.
- [NIMH: Psychotherapies](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/psychotherapies): what psychotherapy involves and how approaches differ.
- [Beck Institute: History of CBT](https://cares.beckinstitute.org/about-cbt/history-of-cbt/): historical development of cognitive therapy.
- [Connecticut DMHAS: DBT](https://portal.ct.gov/dmhas/initiatives/evidence-based/dbt): acceptance, change and the origin of DBT.

Items teach terminology and conceptual distinctions. Illustrations of exposure, ERP and other clinical interventions explain the ideas rather than prescribe a treatment plan.

## Mental health diagnostic cases

The bank contains 120 fictional cases across 40 learning themes. Each question explicitly names **DSM-5-TR** or **ICD-11**. Cases ask for the best-fitting category from four choices, considering symptoms, course, impairment and relevant exclusions; they are not complete clinical assessments. The cases and explanations are original educational writing, not reproduced diagnostic checklists.

- [American Psychiatric Association: What is the DSM?](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/what-is-the-dsm/) and [DSM-5-TR updates](https://www.psychiatry.org/psychiatrists/practice/dsm/updates-to-dsm/updates-to-dsm-5-tr-criteria-text/): classification and edition information.
- [WHO: ICD-11 clinical descriptions and diagnostic requirements](https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/9789240077263): framework reference for ICD-11 cases.
- [NIMH health topics](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics): depression, bipolar disorder, anxiety, OCD, eating disorders, schizophrenia, ADHD and autism.
- [US National Center for PTSD: PTSD and DSM-5](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/professional/treat/essentials/dsm5_ptsd.asp) and [acute stress disorder](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/professional/treat/essentials/acute_stress_disorder.asp): trauma-related patterns and time courses.
- [APA: prolonged grief disorder](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/prolonged-grief-disorder): adult DSM-5-TR duration and impairment distinctions.
- [APA: dissociative disorders](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/dissociative-disorders/dissociative-disorders) and [somatic symptom disorder](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/somatic-symptom-disorder/what-is-somatic-symptom-disorder/): differential concepts.
- [WHO: gaming disorder](https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/frequently-asked-questions/gaming-disorder) and [NHLBI: insomnia](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/insomnia): functional impact and course.

Classification differences are stated rather than merged. In particular, ICD-11 complex PTSD is not presented as a separate DSM-5-TR diagnosis, and the adult prolonged-grief examples distinguish the ICD-11 six-month minimum from the DSM-5-TR twelve-month minimum. Time alone does not establish either diagnosis. Distress, cultural context, impairment and alternative explanations remain important.

The expanded neuropsychology bank also includes progressive language and cognitive presentations. Further background: [NIA overview of dementias](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/alzheimers-and-dementia/understanding-different-types-dementia), [PPA assessment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6464628/) and [posterior cortical atrophy consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5788455/).

Source URLs remain in every TXT record for editorial traceability. **They are not rendered as “Explore the topic” links in either email format.**

## Irish

- [Teanglann: dictionary, grammar and pronunciation](https://www.teanglann.ie/en/).
- [Teanglann: bí](https://www.teanglann.ie/en/fgb/b%C3%AD): present, past, future and habitual forms.
- [Foclóir: English–Irish dictionary](https://www.focloir.ie/): further word and phrase lookup.

Instructions are in English. Tasks use short practical sentences at approximately A2/B1, with standard forms and explanations in English. Regional variants are not automatically wrong. Pronunciation practice is directed to native audio rather than invented English phonetic spellings.

## Music

- [musictheory.net: Lessons](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons): note values, metres, intervals, scales, chords and inversions.

Pitch-distance questions state equal temperament where useful. Rhythmic questions specify the time signature and beat unit. Irish/British terminology is paired with American terminology where needed, for example crotchet / quarter note.

## Spain: weekly news in Spanish

- [EL PAÍS: Spain](https://elpais.com/espana/) and its [official RSS directory](https://elpais.com/info/rss/).
- [elDiario.es: politics](https://www.eldiario.es/politica/).
- [Europa Press: national news](https://www.europapress.es/nacional/).

Five recent national stories are selected using cross-outlet coverage, public-interest terms and publication date. Headlines, dates, source labels and availability notes are in Spanish. Learning sections remain in English. This is an automated selection from the available feeds within the past seven days, not an exhaustive editorial ranking of all events in Spain.

The preview snapshot was fetched from the Spanish RSS feeds on 21 November 2026. It contains five source-linked headlines with publication timestamps. Exact records are in `previews/news_snapshot.json`. The snapshot is only a preview input: live sends fetch fresh RSS. The example archive does not reconstruct older weeks of news.

## Difficulty revision

All six banks retain 120 questions, stable IDs and four answer positions. Distractors now emphasise nearby concepts, differential diagnoses, related grammatical forms and common musical errors. Neuropsychology vignettes include additional discriminating findings where needed. Irish remains approximately A2/B1 and music remains introductory.

Neuroscience has a more substantial revision: applied experimental scenarios replace simple recall prompts, with attention to prediction errors, receptor/circuit context, shunting inhibition, synaptic mechanisms, method selection, confounds and limits on causal inference. Some correct answer content was updated to match the revised question. Classification names in mental-health cases retain the stated DSM-5-TR or ICD-11 framework.
