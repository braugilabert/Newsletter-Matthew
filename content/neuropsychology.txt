ID: neuropsychology-001
TITLE: Words that will not come
QUESTION: After a left middle cerebral artery stroke affecting the inferior frontal language network, a right-handed teacher speaks in short, effortful phrases. Simple comprehension is relatively preserved, and the right arm is weak. Which syndrome and network fit best?
A: Wernicke-type aphasia; posterior temporal language network
B: Hemispatial neglect; right attention network
C: Broca-type aphasia; dominant frontal language network
D: Pure word deafness; bilateral auditory-language networks
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Effortful, agrammatic language with relatively preserved simple comprehension suggests a nonfluent aphasia. Broca-type presentations often involve a wider frontal network than one small cortical area.
EXAMPLE: Compare: fluent speech with poor comprehension points towards a posterior language-network syndrome. Try describing the contrast in one sentence.

QUESTION: After a witnessed focal seizure affecting the left arm, a person has temporary left-arm weakness. Emergency assessment excludes an acute stroke, and strength returns over the following hours. Which conclusion would be unsafe?
A: Using the event timeline in the differential
B: Recognising that focal weakness has several possible causes
C: Assuming new focal weakness never requires stroke assessment if a seizure occurred
D: Reviewing EEG and imaging alongside examination
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Todd paresis is transient focal weakness following a seizure. New weakness still requires urgent assessment because stroke and seizure can coexist or resemble one another.
EXAMPLE: The temporal sequence matters: focal motor activity followed by temporary weakness differs from concluding that any weak limb is postictal.

ID: neuropsychology-119
TITLE: Attention in a busy room
QUESTION: After a traumatic brain injury, an adult follows instructions in a quiet room but misses important information when several people speak or unrelated noises compete for attention. Which observation helps establish the impact of distraction?
A: A normal resting pulse
B: A single correct answer in silence
C: Performance worsens substantially when irrelevant competing stimuli are added
D: Normal ability to identify red and blue
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Selective attention is the ability to prioritise relevant input amid competing information. A noisy setting adds demands not captured by a quiet single-stimulus test.
EXAMPLE: Compare following a conversation in a quiet room with following it amid several competing conversations.
SOURCE: https://openstax.org/details/books/introduction-behavioral-neuroscience

ID: neuropsychology-120
TITLE: Remembering to do it later
QUESTION: Following frontal traumatic injury, a person remembers discussing an appointment when prompted but repeatedly fails to act on that intention at the right time without reminders. Which distinction is correct?
A: Any missed appointment proves total amnesia
B: Recalling an intention does not guarantee initiating it at the appropriate later moment
C: Prospective memory never involves executive control
D: Prospective memory is identical to visual acuity
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Prospective memory concerns carrying out an intended action at a later time or in response to a cue. It draws on memory and executive processes, beyond simply recalling past events.
EXAMPLE: Remembering yesterday’s appointment is retrospective memory; remembering to attend tomorrow’s appointment is prospective memory.
SOURCE: https://openstax.org/details/books/introduction-behavioral-neuroscience
