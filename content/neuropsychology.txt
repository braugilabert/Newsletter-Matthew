ID: neuropsychology-001
TITLE: Words that will not come
QUESTION: After a left middle cerebral artery stroke affecting the inferior frontal language network, a right-handed teacher speaks in short, effortful phrases. Simple comprehension is relatively preserved, and the right arm is weak. Phrase repetition is also impaired. Which syndrome and network fit best?
A: Transcortical motor aphasia; dominant frontal initiation network
B: Conduction aphasia; dominant dorsal language pathways
C: Broca-type aphasia; dominant frontal language network
D: Apraxia of speech without aphasia; dominant premotor speech network
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Effortful, agrammatic language with relatively preserved simple comprehension suggests a nonfluent aphasia. Broca-type presentations often involve a wider frontal network than one small cortical area.
EXAMPLE: Compare: fluent speech with poor comprehension points towards a posterior language-network syndrome. Try describing the contrast in one sentence.
