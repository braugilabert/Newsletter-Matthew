ID: neuropsychology-001
TITLE: Words that will not come
QUESTION: After a left middle cerebral artery stroke affecting the inferior frontal language network, a right-handed teacher speaks in short, effortful phrases. Simple comprehension is relatively preserved, and the right arm is weak. Which syndrome and network fit best?
A: Wernicke-type aphasia; posterior temporal language network
B: Hemispatial neglect; right attention network
C: Broca-type aphasia; dominant frontal language network
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Effortful, agrammatic language with relatively preserved simple comprehension suggests a nonfluent aphasia. Broca-type presentations often involve a wider frontal network than one small cortical area.
EXAMPLE: Compare: fluent speech with poor comprehension points towards a posterior language-network syndrome. Try describing the contrast in one sentence.

ID: neuropsychology-002
TITLE: Fluent but hard to follow
QUESTION: Following a left posterior temporal stroke, a patient speaks easily but uses invented words and does not understand simple spoken instructions. Hearing is intact. Which pairing is most likely?
A: Wernicke-type aphasia; dominant posterior temporal language network
B: Cerebellar ataxia; cerebellar hemisphere
C: Broca-type aphasia; dominant inferior frontal network
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Fluency does not guarantee meaningful language. Impaired comprehension plus fluent, disorganised speech fits a Wernicke-type pattern, with variable involvement of nearby language regions.
EXAMPLE: A person says a long sentence but cannot follow 'close your eyes': test language comprehension rather than assuming a hearing problem.

ID: neuropsychology-003
TITLE: Half a plate
QUESTION: After a right parietal stroke, a patient eats food only from the right half of the plate and ignores people on the left. Basic visual fields are intact on examination. Which pairing fits?
A: Left homonymous hemianopia; right primary visual cortex
B: Broca-type aphasia; left inferior frontal network
C: Left hemispatial neglect; right frontoparietal attention network
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Neglect is impaired attention to contralesional space, not simply loss of vision. Right parietal and connected attention networks are commonly involved.
EXAMPLE: A clock drawing with all numbers crowded onto the right can suggest neglect. Contrast it with a visual-field defect, which needs separate testing.
