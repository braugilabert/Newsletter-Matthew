ID: psychotherapy-001
TITLE: CBT's central link
QUESTION: Which relationship is central to a standard CBT formulation?
A: Changing thought content is the necessary first step before behaviour can alter emotion
B: Thoughts, emotions and behaviours influence one another in context
C: Distress is maintained chiefly by distorted thoughts rather than reciprocal behavioural and contextual processes
D: Behaviour changes emotion directly, while interpretations are treated as secondary consequences
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: CBT examines patterns that maintain distress and tests more helpful alternatives. The aim is balanced and workable thinking, not forced positivity.
EXAMPLE: Situation: no reply to a message. Thought: 'They dislike me.' Alternative: 'They may be busy.' Behaviour: wait rather than send repeated messages.

TITLE: Choosing a valued direction
QUESTION: Which is expressed as an ongoing value rather than a completed goal?
A: Read one chapter by Friday
B: Feel confident when learning something new
C: Obtain a certificate at the end of the course
D: Being a curious and engaged learner
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: ACT uses values to guide ongoing action, while committed action translates that direction into workable steps. Values are chosen directions rather than a competition for the most admirable label.
EXAMPLE: A value of being a caring friend might guide one small action, such as checking in with someone.
