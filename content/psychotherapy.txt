ID: psychotherapy-001
TITLE: CBT's central link
QUESTION: Which relationship is central to a standard CBT formulation?
A: Thoughts must always be positive to be useful
B: Thoughts, emotions and behaviours influence one another in context
C: Only childhood dreams influence present behaviour
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: CBT examines patterns that maintain distress and tests more helpful alternatives. The aim is balanced and workable thinking, not forced positivity.
EXAMPLE: Situation: no reply to a message. Thought: 'They dislike me.' Alternative: 'They may be busy.' Behaviour: wait rather than send repeated messages.

B: A guaranteed objective fact

ID: psychotherapy-014
TITLE: Negative reinforcement
QUESTION: A behaviour increases because it removes an unpleasant experience. What is the learning term?
A: Extinction by definition
B: Negative reinforcement
C: Positive punishment
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Negative means something is removed; reinforcement means the behaviour becomes more likely. It does not mean the behaviour is morally bad.
EXAMPLE: Fastening a seat belt stops an annoying alarm, making fastening more likely. That illustrates negative reinforcement.

QUESTION: A behaviour becomes more frequent after a rewarding consequence is added. What is this?
A: Repression
B: Positive reinforcement

TITLE: Defusion
QUESTION: Which phrase best illustrates cognitive defusion in ACT?
A: I must prove I will never fail at anything
B: I am noticing the thought that I will fail
C: This thought is a command I must obey
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Defusion changes the relationship to a thought rather than automatically arguing with its content. A thought can be noticed as an event in the mind.

ID: psychotherapy-026
TITLE: Values and goals
QUESTION: Which distinction fits ACT terminology?
A: A value is an ongoing direction; a goal is a specific achievable outcome
B: A value is always completed once and never revisited
C: Goals are feelings and values are test scores
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Values guide patterns of action over time, while goals can mark concrete steps. The same goal can serve different values for different people.
EXAMPLE: Value: learning. Goal: practise three music questions this week. Completing the goal does not finish the value of learning.
