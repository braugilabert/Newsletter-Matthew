TITLE: The journey that actually happened
QUESTION: Read: Bhí mé chun an traein a thógáil, ach cuireadh ar ceal í. Thug mo dheirfiúr síob dom, mar ní raibh bus ar bith ag dul ag an am sin. How did the speaker actually travel?
A: They took a later train
B: They caught the bus
C: Their sister gave them a lift
D: They walked to the destination
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Separate an earlier plan from the action taken after ach (but). A transport mentioned first is not necessarily the one actually used; the final completed action resolves the question.
EXAMPLE: Bhí mé chun siúl, ach thóg mé an bus = I was going to walk, but I took the bus.

TITLE: Looking for something lost
QUESTION: Read: Níor thug mé an cóta liom ón oifig inné. Tá mé ag súil go bhfuil sé fós ann, ach níor chuir mé glaoch orthu fós. What is known rather than merely hoped?
A: The office has confirmed it still has the coat
B: The coat was not brought home from the office
C: The coat has already been collected
D: The coat was definitely lost somewhere else
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Track the difference between a place already searched and a place proposed for the next search. Fós can indicate that the search remains unresolved.
EXAMPLE: D’fhéach mé sa charr, ach ní bhfuair mé é = I looked in the car, but did not find it.

ID: irish-116
TITLE: An ongoing duration
QUESTION: Read: Tá mé anseo ó bhí mé i mo pháiste. Which meaning is closest?
A: I was here only during childhood
B: I will come here with my child
C: I was here before becoming a parent, but have left
D: I have been here since I was a child
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: A present activity with le and a duration commonly indicates that it began earlier and continues now. A completed past period or a future plan has a different time interpretation.
EXAMPLE: Tá mé ag obair anseo le dhá bhliain = I have been working here for two years.

ID: irish-117
TITLE: Changing a volunteer rota
QUESTION: Read: Is féidir liom cabhrú ar maidin, ach caithfidh mé imeacht faoi 1.00. Beidh duine eile ag teastáil don tráthnóna. What cover is still needed?
A: Someone for the morning only
B: Someone for the afternoon
C: Nobody, because the speaker can stay all day
D: Someone only before the speaker arrives
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Work out both the original responsibility and the agreed swap. A request is not the same as a completed agreement unless the reply confirms it.
EXAMPLE: Oibreoidh mé Dé Máirt ina áit = I will work Tuesday instead.

ID: irish-118
TITLE: The comparison must match the facts
QUESTION: Read: Níl an dara turas chomh fada leis an gcéad cheann, ach tógann sé níos mó ama. Which distinction is made?
A: The second journey is longer in distance and quicker
B: The second journey is shorter in distance but takes longer
C: Both journeys have the same distance and duration
D: The second journey is shorter and takes less time
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Níos saoire is cheaper; níos daoire is more expensive. In a comparison with ná, reversing the subjects reverses which thing is cheaper or larger.
EXAMPLE: Tá A níos saoire ná B expresses A costing less than B, not the other way round.

ID: irish-119
TITLE: A message about taking a break
QUESTION: Read: Tá mé ag obair níos lú an tseachtain seo chun níos mó ama a chaitheamh leis an teaghlach. Fillfidh mé ar an ngnáthchlár an tseachtain seo chugainn. What is stated?
A: The speaker has left the job
B: The normal schedule starts for the first time today
C: The family will be away next week
D: The reduction in work is temporary
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Read the reason for a temporary change and whether the person is stopping completely or reducing an activity. Time expressions limit the claim being made.
EXAMPLE: An tseachtain seo amháin = this week only; it does not describe a permanent decision.

ID: irish-120
TITLE: A reason and a concession
QUESTION: Read: Thóg mé an bus chun teacht in am, ach bhí moill air agus bhí mé déanach fós. What is the contrast?
A: The speaker intended to arrive late and succeeded
B: The intended purpose was punctuality, but the result was lateness
C: The bus was on time but the speaker missed it
D: The speaker arrived early because the bus was delayed
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Mar and toisc introduce reasons; cé go introduces a concession. The relationship between clauses matters: although does not mean because, and a reason does not automatically describe the action taken.
EXAMPLE: Cé go raibh sé tuirseach, chuaigh sé amach = Although he was tired, he went out.
SOURCE: https://www.teanglann.ie/en/
