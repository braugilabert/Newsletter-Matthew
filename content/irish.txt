TITLE: How are you?
QUESTION: Choose the ordinary Irish question meaning 'How are you?'
A: Conas a bhí tú?
B: Conas atá sí?
C: Conas atá tú?
D: Conas a bheidh tú?
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Conas asks how. Cá asks where. Cé asks who. Conas atá tú? is a common standard form, with regional alternatives in everyday speech.
EXAMPLE: Mini-dialogue: Conas atá tú? Tá mé go maith, go raibh maith agat. Meaning: How are you? I am well, thank you.

QUESTION: Choose the negative reply to An raibh tú ann? (Were you there?)
A: Níl.
B: Ní bheidh.
C: Ní raibh.
D: Ní bheadh.
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Bhí is the affirmative past of bí; ní raibh is negative and an raibh introduces a question. Raibh is used in the dependent past forms here.
EXAMPLE: An raibh tú sa bhaile? Bhí. Were you at home? Yes.

A: Beidh
B: Bheadh
