ID: neuroscience-001
TITLE: Dopamine and an unexpected outcome
QUESTION: In a classic reward-learning experiment, a phasic dopamine response shifts from an unexpected reward to the cue predicting it. When the predicted reward is omitted, activity briefly falls at its expected delivery time. Which interpretation best fits this pattern?
A: The predicted value of the reward itself, independent of the outcome
B: An unsigned surprise signal that should increase after either omission or delivery
C: A signed reward prediction error rather than a direct readout of pleasure
D: A sensory response to reward consumption that is merely delayed by the cue
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: In the classic prediction-error account, better-than-expected outcomes produce a positive error, while an omitted expected reward produces a negative error. Learned prediction shifts the response towards the cue; this is not a universal description of every dopamine neuron.
EXAMPLE: If a cue reliably predicts coffee, the relevant learning signal may move towards the cue; unexpectedly missing the coffee can generate a negative error.

EXPLANATION: A transmitter’s effect depends on receptor properties, ionic gradients and the network. Excitation and inhibition are functional outcomes, not universal labels that can be inferred from a chemical name alone.
EXAMPLE: Inhibiting an inhibitory interneuron may increase the activity of another cell: this is disinhibition.

TITLE: Sleep and remembering
QUESTION: Two memory tests differ in fatigue at retrieval. Why must alertness be considered before interpreting a lower score as loss of stored information?
A: A lower test score after waking demonstrates that stored information was lost
B: Time since learning determines retrieval accuracy independently of arousal
C: Alertness can affect performance independently of stored knowledge
D: Matching total sleep duration removes the need to consider alertness at testing
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Sleep can support aspects of memory processing, but effects depend on the task, sleep conditions and timing. Memory consolidation is not confined to one sleep stage or a single mechanism.
EXAMPLE: Compare performance after matched intervals with and without sleep while controlling for time of day and fatigue.
