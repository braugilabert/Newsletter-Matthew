ID: mental_health-001
TITLE: Three weeks unlike the usual self
QUESTION: DSM-5-TR. Medical and substance-related explanations have been assessed and excluded. For three weeks, an adult has depressed mood, no enjoyment, early waking, low energy, poor concentration and marked guilt nearly every day, and is missing work. There is no history of mania or hypomania. Which diagnosis best fits this fictional case?
A: Persistent depressive disorder
B: Bipolar II disorder
C: Major depressive disorder
D: Adjustment disorder with depressed mood
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: A major depressive episode involves sustained depressed mood and/or loss of interest with a sufficient accompanying symptom pattern, impairment and relevant exclusions. A history of mania or hypomania changes the diagnostic differential.
EXAMPLE: Distinguish a sustained impairing syndrome from a single sad day. A vignette supports a best-fit answer, not a complete clinical assessment.
